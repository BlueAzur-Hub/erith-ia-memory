From 70666 to a new machine

More than a modification.

Rising Phoenix began with the idea behind LEGO NINJAGO set 70666, The Golden Dragon. The original silhouette immediately suggested something larger: a mechanical creature with a stronger frame, a visible energy system and enough space to carry a small structure on its back.

Over four months, the physical model evolved through repeated rebuilding, targeted part purchases and mechanical refinement. The result is a long, articulated display dragon that keeps the gold, dark green, black, dark gray and translucent green language of the source while developing its own architecture.

The published project deliberately stops at three official levels. That keeps the build modular and gives each builder a choice: begin with the more accessible Dragon Speeder, add Master Wu’s Temple, or continue to the full rear propulsion configuration.

The finished physical model was then rebuilt digitally in BrickLink Studio so its structure, modular logic and construction sequence could be documented as a complete instruction guide.

Engineered to be real

Not only a Studio model.

Rising Phoenix exists as a complete physical build. The final shape was refined with real bricks, real weight, real articulation and real display constraints before the digital model and instructions were completed.

Decorative identity for Master Wu

Master Wu's Temple

The mechanical dragon is the hero of Rising Phoenix. The small Temple is a display module built around Master Wu: his golden hat can be stored on the structure together with his weapons, while the blossoms and hanging details add a decorative Japanese-inspired signature.

Lloyd is presented as the engineer behind the mechanical dragons, while Wu is the intended rider of this one. The published presentation uses that pairing to tell the story, but builders are free to use any minifigures they prefer.

Four months of creation

The model was discovered by rebuilding it.

The final design did not appear in a single session. It grew through physical experiments, dismantling, targeted parts and repeated changes of proportion.
